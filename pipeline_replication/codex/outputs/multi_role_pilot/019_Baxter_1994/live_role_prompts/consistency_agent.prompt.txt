## 1. Paper Design

- **Experimental system:** Outdoor open-top chambers (OTC), University of Wales Bangor (Snowdonia)
- **CO2 levels:** Ambient 340 µmol mol⁻¹ (control) vs. elevated 680 µmol mol⁻¹ (treatment)
- **Species (3 C3 grasses):**
  - *Agrostis capillaris* L. - single harvest at 43 d
  - *Poa alpina* L. - two harvests: 56 d and 105 d
  - *Festuca vivipara* L. - two harvests: 65 d and 189 d
- **Replication:** n = 4 pots per treatment
- **Growth medium:** Unistesilized compost (0.75 dm³ pots), N:P:K at 5.1%:3.1%:8.3%; *P. alpina* and *F. vivipara* received supplemental fertilization (Long Ashton solution) after 60 d
- **Key design feature:** This paper is Part II of a paired study. Part I (Baxter et al., 1994, same year, same volume pp. 305-115) reported growth and dry matter partitioning. Part II reports nutrient data.

**Key measurement complexity:** All nutrient concentrations and content values are expressed **per unit structural dry weight** (total DW minus non-structural carbohydrate content). This is an unusual and non-standard basis that differs from conventional total-DW concentration used by most other papers in the Loladze 2014 dataset.

**Tissue breakdown:** Leaf blade (L), leaf sheath (S), and root (R) were measured separately over time. Concentrations of N, P, K, Mg, and Ca were determined in all three fractions for all three species.

**Notable verification flags:** The verification system flagged 10 of 15 observations as suspected T/C swaps for *A. capillaris* and *P. alpina*. This is because the paper reports positive nutrient uptake effects under elevated CO2 for these species (more plant growth = more total nutrient uptake), while the Loladze GT records negative concentration effects in foliar tissue. Only the *F. vivipara* observations pass the direction check because F. vivipara grew less under elevated CO2, so both its total content and concentrations declined.
