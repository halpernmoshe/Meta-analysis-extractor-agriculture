# Benchmark Representativeness Note

The benchmark meta-analyses should not automatically be treated as more representative than the pipeline-derived sample.

They are useful as external reference points because they are:

- published syntheses
- manually curated
- established in the literature

But that does **not** imply that they are the uniquely correct or fully representative population-level truth.

## Better Framing

The comparison should be framed as:

- **benchmark comparison**, not benchmark supremacy
- **agreement / disagreement with the published reference synthesis**
- **differences in corpus composition or estimand definition**

rather than:

- “our pipeline failed because the benchmark is the true representative sample”

## Why This Matters

When the pipeline and the benchmark differ, several interpretations remain possible:

1. the pipeline extracted or filtered the wrong rows
2. the accessible corpus differs from the benchmark corpus
3. the benchmark itself reflects a different crop, climate, management, or study mix
4. the pipeline and benchmark are estimating slightly different constructs

The correct paper claim is therefore:

- the benchmark is the comparison target
- disagreement is informative
- disagreement does not prove the benchmark is more representative

## Stronger Paper Language

Prefer wording like:

- “deviated from the published benchmark synthesis”
- “did not reproduce the published pooled effect”
- “disagreement may reflect extraction/filtering error, corpus composition differences, or benchmark-sample differences”

Avoid wording like:

- “the benchmark is the representative truth and our sample is biased”

unless there is independent evidence for that stronger claim.
