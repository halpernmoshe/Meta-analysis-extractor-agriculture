# 008_Campbell_2002 - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Interactions between atmospheric CO2 concentration and phosphorus nutrition on the formation of proteoid roots in white lupin (Lupinus albus L.)
- **Authors**: C. D. Campbell & R. F. Sage (2002)
- **Journal**: Plant, Cell and Environment, 25, 1051-1059
- **Species**: Lupinus albus L. (white lupin)
- **Experiment**: Hydroponic, controlled environment chambers
- **CO2 levels**: 200 (sub-ambient), 410 (ambient), 750 (elevated) umol/mol
- **P treatments**: Low P (0.69 uM), High P (250 uM)
- **Harvest**: 22 days after germination
- **Replicates**: n = 3 experiments (6 samples per replicate)

## Available Element Data
This paper reports only **phosphorus (P)** concentration data (no other mineral elements measured).

### Data Sources
- **Table 1**: Total plant P content (mg plant-1) and proportion of P in roots - NOT concentration data
- **Figure 4**: Tissue P concentration (% DW) by tissue type, CO2 level, and P treatment - PRIMARY SOURCE

### Notes on Other Elements
- No data on N, K, Ca, Mg, Fe, Zn, Mn, Cu, S, B, C, Na, Mo, Si or any other elements
- The study focused specifically on P nutrition and proteoid root formation

## Figure 4 Reading Notes
- Values estimated from bar/line chart (Figure 4)
- Fig 4a (low P): y-axis is tissue P content (% DW), range 0-0.30
  - Leaf P at 200 ppm ~0.10 (significantly higher than 410 or 750)
  - Leaf P at 410 ppm ~0.04, at 750 ppm ~0.04 (not significantly different)
  - Normal root P relatively stable across CO2 levels (~0.18-0.20)
  - Proteoid root P highest at 200 (~0.30), declines at 410 (~0.25), 750 (~0.22)
  - Text states: "proteoid roots produced at low [CO2] had 30% higher [P] than proteoid roots from plants grown at higher [CO2]"
- Fig 4b (high P): y-axis range 0-4
  - Leaf P ~0.85-1.0 across CO2 levels
  - Normal root P ~2.8-3.0 across CO2 levels
  - Proteoid root P ~2.3-2.5 across CO2 levels
  - Text says: "no significant difference existed between proteoid and non-proteoid roots in terms of [P]"

## Key Context from Text
- "The effect of [CO2] on [P] was significant only in the low-P-treated plants"
- "Leaves of low-[CO2]-grown plants had more than twice the P concentration of leaves of either the ambient or high-[CO2]-grown plants (P < 0.01)"
- The smaller low-CO2-grown plants had higher [P] because total P from seed reserves was diluted less
- At high P, [CO2] had no significant effect on tissue P concentration
- Total plant P content (Table 1): Low P ~1.6-1.9 mg/plant regardless of CO2; High P increased from 14.3 to 21.1 mg/plant with rising CO2 (due to plant size increase)

## Summary
- 6 total observations extracted (P concentration only)
- P is the only element with concentration data in this paper
- Effect of elevated CO2 on P concentration is generally small to moderate
- Most significant effect is dilution of P in leaves at low P supply when CO2 increases
- The main story of this paper is about proteoid root production, not mineral concentration changes
