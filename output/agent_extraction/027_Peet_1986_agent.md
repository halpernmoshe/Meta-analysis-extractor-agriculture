# 027_Peet_1986 - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Acclimation to High CO2 in Monoecious Cucumbers. II. Carbon Exchange Rates, Enzyme Activities, and Starch and Nutrient Concentrations
- **Authors**: Peet MM, Huber SC, Patterson DT
- **Year**: 1986
- **Journal**: Plant Physiology 80: 63-67
- **Species**: Cucumis sativus cv Chipper (monoecious cucumber)
- **CO2 Levels**: 350 (control) vs 1000 (treatment) ul/L
- **Experiment**: Controlled environment chambers, Duke University Phytotron

## Data Sources
- **Figure 4**: Bar chart showing nutrient concentrations (% dry weight) and starch at three growth stages
- Elements measured: N, P, K, Ca, Mg (5 elements)
- Growth stages: Vegetative (d36), Flowering (d43), Fruiting (d60)
- n = 8 plants per treatment

## Notes
- All nutrient data from Figure 4 only (no tables with nutrient values)
- Text states normal ranges: N 3.5-4.5%, P 0.35-0.65%, K 3.5-5.0%, Ca 1.5-4.0%, Mg 0.2-0.4%
- All values within or slightly above normal range
- Calcium showed the greatest reduction under elevated CO2
- By fruiting, nutrients were similar in both treatments
- Leaf necrosis observed in enriched plants (resembled K deficiency)
- Starch concentrations higher in 1000 ul/L plants (associated with lower nutrients)

- **15 observations** across 5 elements and 3 growth stages
- All effects negative or zero (elevated CO2 reduced or maintained nutrient concentrations)
