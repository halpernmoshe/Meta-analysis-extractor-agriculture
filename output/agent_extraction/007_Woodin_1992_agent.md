# 007_Woodin_1992 - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Nutrient limitation of the long term response of heather [Calluna vulgaris (L.) Hull] to CO2 enrichment
- **Authors**: Woodin, Graham, Killick, Skiba, Cresser
- **Journal**: New Phytologist (1992), 122, 635-642
- **Species**: Calluna vulgaris (heather)
- **Experiment**: Growth chamber, 27 months
- **CO2 treatments**: Ambient (370 ppm), +100 ppm (470 ppm), +200 ppm (570 ppm)
- **n per treatment**: 10 plants
- **Tissues analyzed**: Leaf and stem (roots could not be extracted from peat)
- **Elements measured**: N, P, K, Ca, Mg (concentrations); also C:N ratio in leaf

## Data Source
All concentration data from **Figure 3a** (bar charts with 95% CI). No numerical table of concentrations provided.
Text states treatment effects significant for N, P, Mg (all P < 0.001), K and Ca (P < 0.01) via ANOVA.

## Additional info from text
- C:N ratio (leaf): 40.1 (370 ppm), 44.3 (470 ppm), 51.7 (570 ppm) - P < 0.05
- Total pools (Figure 3b): No significant effect on N, P, K pools; Ca and Mg pools increased (P < 0.05)
- Leaf concentrations of N, P, K all "significantly reduced in both high CO2 treatments"
- Stem: P decreased, K and Mg no significant effect, N and Ca increased

## Key Patterns
- **Leaf tissue**: All 5 elements show decreased concentrations under elevated CO2 (nutrient dilution)
- **Stem tissue**: Mixed responses - N and Ca increased, P decreased, K and Mg largely unchanged
- Dilution effect is dose-dependent: larger decreases at 570 ppm than 470 ppm for leaf tissue
- The paper's main conclusion: nutrient uptake did NOT increase with growth, causing dilution
- Average leaf effect at 470 ppm: -27.9% across N, P, K, Ca, Mg
- Average leaf effect at 570 ppm: -40.2% across N, P, K, Ca, Mg

- All values estimated from bar charts (Figure 3a) - no exact numerical data in paper
- Error bars (95% CI) visible but not transcribed numerically
- Units: mg g-1 dry weight for all elements
