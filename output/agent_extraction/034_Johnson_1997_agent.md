# 034_Johnson_1997 (actually Johnson et al. 2003) - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: The effects of elevated CO2 on nutrient distribution in a fire-adapted scrub oak forest
- **Authors**: Johnson, D.W., Hungate, B.A., Dijkstra, P., Hymus, G., Hinkle, C.R., Stiling, P., Drake, B.G.
- **Journal**: Ecological Applications 13(5): 1388-1399 (2003)
- **Species**: Quercus myrtifolia (myrtle oak), Quercus geminata (sand live oak)
- **Experiment**: Open-top chambers (OTCs), 16 chambers total
- **CO2 levels**: Ambient vs. ambient + 350 uL/L (elevated)
- **Duration**: 5 years (1996-2001), site burned Aug 1995
- **Replication**: n=8 per treatment
- **Location**: Merritt Island, Kennedy Space Center, Florida

## Data Sources

### Table 2 - Biomass and nutrient CONTENTS (kg/ha or g/ha) by species

## Notes
- Significance: *P<0.05, **P<0.01, ***P<0.001
- The paper reports both concentrations (Table 3) and total contents (Table 2)
- For Loladze meta-analysis, Table 3 concentration data is the relevant data
- Table 2 content data reflects both concentration changes AND biomass changes
- Weighted mean = biomass-weighted average of foliage and stem concentrations
- N consistently declined in foliage under elevated CO2 (both species)
- Mn consistently increased under elevated CO2 (especially in litterfall: +53%)
- B consistently decreased under elevated CO2
- S showed large decreases, especially in Q. myrtifolia (-24% foliage, -33% weighted)
