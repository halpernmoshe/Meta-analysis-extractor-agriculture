# 046_Porter_1984 - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Acclimation to High CO2 in Bean: Carbonic Anhydrase and Ribulose Bisphosphate Carboxylase
- **Authors**: Porter MA, Grodzinski B
- **Year**: 1984
- **Journal**: Plant Physiology 74: 413-416
- **Species**: Phaseolus vulgaris L. cv Seafarer (bean)
- **Experiment Type**: Growth chamber
- **CO2 Levels**: 330 ul/l (ambient) vs 1200 ul/l (elevated)
- **Duration**: 7 and 14 days of treatment

## Data Source
All mineral concentration data from **Table II** - "Effect of High or Ambient CO2 on Vegetative Mineral Composition"

Units are % dry weight. The paper states leaves from high CO2 plants contained "approximately 75 and 65% of the control levels of N, P, K, Ca, and Mg after 7 and 14 d of treatment, respectively."

## Extracted Observations (10 total)

| Element | Tissue | High CO2 | Ambient CO2 | Effect (%) | Significance |

## Notes
- All 5 mineral elements showed significant declines under elevated CO2 at both time points
- The decline was more severe at 14 days than at 7 days for all elements
- Sample size (n) not explicitly stated for mineral data; the paper mentions "four replications" for enzyme data
- Table I (growth parameters) and Table III (enzyme activities by leaf position) were not extracted as they do not contain mineral concentration data
- Table IV (glycolate oxidation) was not extracted - enzyme activity data, not mineral concentrations
- Figure 1 shows enzyme activity time courses (carbonic anhydrase, RuBP carboxylase, soluble protein) - not mineral data
