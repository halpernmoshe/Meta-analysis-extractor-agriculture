# 050_Polley_2011 - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: CO2 enrichment increases element concentrations in grass mixtures by changing species abundances
- **Authors**: H. Wayne Polley, Philip A. Fay, Virginia L. Jin, Gerald F. Combs Jr.
- **Year**: 2011
- **Journal**: Plant Ecology 212:945-957
- **Species**: Bouteloua curtipendula, Schizachyrium scoparium, Sorghastrum nutans (C4 tallgrass prairie grasses)
- **Experiment**: Field CO2 gradient (250-500 umol/mol) in elongated chambers, central Texas

## Key Design Notes
- **Continuous CO2 gradient**, not discrete ambient/elevated treatments
- CO2 ranges from ~250 (subambient) to ~500 umol/mol along chamber length
- Three soil types: Austin (Mollisol), Bastrop (sandy loam Alfisol), Houston Black (Vertisol)
- Three C4 grass species sampled in June 2008 and June 2009
- 10 elements measured: C, N, K, Ca, P, Mg, Zn, Mn, Cu, Fe (Fe only in 2009)

## Data Sources

### Tables 1 & 2: Species-level and soil-level means
These tables show mean element concentrations averaged across the ENTIRE CO2 gradient, broken down by soil type and by species. They do NOT represent treatment vs. control comparisons -- they are means pooled across all CO2 levels. Therefore they are not directly usable for effect size calculation.

### Table 3: Combined biomass concentrations at specific CO2 levels (PRIMARY SOURCE)
This table provides element concentrations in the combined biomass of three grasses on the **Austin soil** at five CO2 levels (280, 330, 380, 430, 480 umol/mol) for 2008 and 2009. This is the best source for treatment vs. control comparisons.

- **Control**: 280 umol/mol (subambient, pre-Industrial level)
- **Elevated**: 480 umol/mol (elevated, near double pre-Industrial)

## Important Context
- The increases in K, Mg, and Mn in combined biomass at high CO2 are driven by **species composition shifts** (Sorghastrum replacing Bouteloua), NOT by increases in element concentrations within individual species.
- CO2 had little direct effect on element concentrations within individual grass species (Tables 1-2 text).
- The paper explicitly states P was the only element that showed a direct CO2 response (decline on Bastrop and Houston soils).
- C and N data are mentioned in the text but not included in Table 3 (only C% increased from ~41.5 to 42.8% in Sorghastrum and Schizachyrium in 2008).
- Fe data only available for 2009 (Table 2) as species means, not at specific CO2 levels.

## Elements NOT extracted
- **C**: Tables 1-2 show species means across gradient (not treatment vs control); text mentions increase
- **N**: No significant CO2 effect on N (P=0.47 in 2008, P=0.28 in 2009); not in Table 3
- **Fe**: Only in Table 2 (2009 species means across gradient); not at specific CO2 levels
- **Zn**: Only in Tables 1-2 (species means across gradient); not at specific CO2 levels
- **Cu**: Only in Tables 1-2 (species means across gradient); not at specific CO2 levels
