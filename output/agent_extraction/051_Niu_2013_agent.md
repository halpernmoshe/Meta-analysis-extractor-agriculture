# 051_Niu_2013 - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Effect of elevated CO2 on phosphorus nutrition of phosphate-deficient Arabidopsis thaliana (L.) Heynh under different nitrogen forms
- **Authors**: Niu et al. (2013)
- **Journal**: Journal of Experimental Botany, Vol. 64, No. 1, pp. 355-367
- **Species**: Arabidopsis thaliana (wild-type)
- **CO2 levels**: Ambient 350 +/- 50 ul/l, Elevated 800 +/- 50 ul/l
- **Duration**: 7 days CO2 treatment (plants were 5 weeks old)
- **Growth system**: Hydroponic, P-deficient (0.5 uM NaH2PO4)
- **Factorial design**: CO2 x N form (NO3- vs NH4+)
- **Replicates**: n=5 (at least 8 independent replicates per treatment)

## Element Data - Only P concentration reported

This paper focuses exclusively on phosphorus (P) nutrition. No other mineral element concentrations (N, K, Ca, Mg, Fe, Zn, Mn, Cu, S, B, C, Na, Mo, Si) are reported.

### Data Source: Figure 1d,e (bar charts, values estimated)

The text provides explicit percentage changes which anchor the estimates:
- "Elevated CO2 decreased the P concentration in shoots by 27% but did not alter it in the roots of NH4+-supplied plants."
- "Elevated CO2 increased the P concentration by 32 and 21%, respectively, in the shoots and roots of NO3--fed plants."

## Additional P-related data (not concentration)

These are NOT mineral concentration data but related measures:
- **Fig. 1f**: Total P content (mg/plant) - whole plant, not concentration
- **Fig. 1g**: Amount of P absorbed per weight of roots (mg g-1 DW) - uptake measure
- **Fig. 6**: P concentration with SNP/cPTIO treatments (pharmacological, not standard CO2 effect)
- **Fig. 8**: P concentration in nr and atnos1 mutants (not wild-type)
- **Supplementary Fig. S1**: Biomass and P concentration with P-adequate nutrition

## Notes
- All data from figures (bar charts) - no tables with numerical data in paper
- Paper also reports biomass, root morphology, APase activity, gene expression, NR/NOS activity, and NO production, but these are not mineral concentration data
- The significant CO2 x N form interaction is the key finding: eCO2 increases P in NO3- plants but decreases it in NH4+ plants
- Mutant data (Fig. 8) excluded as these are not wild-type responses
