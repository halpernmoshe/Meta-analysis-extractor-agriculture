# 026_Seneweera_1997 - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Growth, grain yield and quality of rice (Oryza sativa L.) in response to elevated CO2 and phosphorus nutrition
- **Authors**: Seneweera, Conroy (1997)
- **Species**: Oryza sativa L. cv. Jarrah (rice)
- **Experiment**: Growth chamber, 350 vs 700 uL CO2/L
- **Design**: Factorial - 6 P levels (0, 30, 60, 120, 240, 480 mg/kg soil) x 2 CO2
- **Replicates**: n=5
- **Location**: Hawkesbury, NSW, Australia

## Data Sources

### Table 1: Chemical composition of sheaths and blades at 56 DAP

- Tissues: Sheaths and Blades
- 6 P levels x 2 CO2 = 12 rows
- **Extracted**: P, Ca, N, Zn (4 elements x 2 tissues x 6 P levels = 48 observations)

## Key Findings
- **Ca increased** under elevated CO2 across all tissues (sheaths, blades, grain)
- **N decreased** in grain (-11.6% to -22.2%) and blades (-3.7% to -16.7%) under elevated CO2
- **Zn decreased** in grain (-5.7% to -11.1%) under elevated CO2
- **Fe decreased** in grain (-9.1% to -50.0%) under elevated CO2, with larger effects at higher P
- **P in grain** showed mixed effects: decreased at medium P levels, unchanged/increased at low and high P
- Sheath N and Zn showed inconsistent patterns (some increases, some decreases)
- Text states: "N reduced by average 5% (sheaths) and 10% (blades)" by CO2 enrichment
- Table 2 LSD values: N=1.0 g/kg, P=0.3 g/kg (p<=0.05)

## Notes
- Figure values (Ca, Zn, Fe in grain) are estimated from graphs and have inherent uncertainty
- Table 1 Starch data was excluded as it is not a mineral element
- The paper also reports grain amylose concentration (Fig 3a) - excluded as not a mineral
- Blade Starch data excluded (also not mineral)
- All table values are pooled means of 5 replicates as stated in paper
