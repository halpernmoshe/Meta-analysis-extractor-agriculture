# 019_Baxter_1994 - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Effects of elevated carbon dioxide on three grass species from montane pasture. II. Nutrient uptake, allocation and efficiency of use
- **Authors**: Baxter R, Gantley M, Ashenden TW, Farrar JF
- **Year**: 1994
- **Journal**: Journal of Experimental Botany, Vol. 45, No. 278, pp. 1267-1278
- **Species**: Agrostis capillaris, Poa alpina, Festuca vivipara
- **CO2 levels**: 340 (ambient) vs 680 (elevated) umol mol-1
- **Design**: Open-top chambers, n=4 replicates
- **Exposure**: A. capillaris 43 d, P. alpina 105 d, F. vivipara 189 d

## Data Sources
- **Table 1**: Total content of N, P, K, Mg, Ca (mg plant-1, extracted dry weight basis) for all three species
- **Table 2**: Photosynthetic N and P use efficiencies (PNUE, PPUE) - not mineral concentrations
- **Figures 1-2**: Tissue-level N, P, K, Mg, Ca concentrations (mg g-1 structural DW) for leaf, sheath, root - bar charts requiring visual estimation
- **Figure 5**: Inflorescence nutrient content for P. alpina (reproductive tissue)

## Important Notes
1. Table 1 data is **total nutrient content per plant** (mg plant-1), NOT tissue concentration
2. The paper states: "There was no systematic reduction of nutrient concentration when expressed per unit structural dry weight at elevated CO2 in A. capillaris and P. alpina"
3. For F. vivipara, total uptake was markedly reduced at elevated CO2 (plant grew less)
4. Text reports F. vivipara uptake reductions: N -73%, P -48%, K -38%, Mg -37%, Ca -45%
5. A. capillaris uptake approximately doubled for N, P, K, Mg, Ca (text says "approximately doubled, and P trebled")
6. P. alpina: N and K uptake unaltered, Ca uptake reduced by half, P and Mg increased

## Tissue Concentration Notes (Figures 1-2, qualitative summary)
The figures show concentration data (mg g-1 structural DW) by tissue (leaf, sheath, root):

### Nitrogen (Fig. 1a-c):
- A. capillaris: No change in any tissue at 56 or 105 d
- P. alpina: No change at 56 d; reduced at 105 d in all tissues
- F. vivipara: Higher in leaf at 65 d (elevated CO2); significantly lower at 189 d in all tissues

### Phosphorus (Fig. 1d-f):
- A. capillaris: No change at 56 or 105 d
- P. alpina: Reduced in roots at 56 and 105 d at elevated CO2
- F. vivipara: ~2x higher in leaf at 65 d (elevated); no difference by 189 d

### Potassium (Fig. 2a-c):
- No significant CO2 effect in A. capillaris or P. alpina
- F. vivipara: K fell markedly in all tissues between 65 and 189 d (no CO2 effect except leaf blade at 65d where ~2x at elevated)

### Magnesium (Fig. 2d-f):
- A. capillaris: No change
- P. alpina: No change
- F. vivipara: Lower in leaf, sheath, root at 65 d (elevated CO2), differences vanished by 189 d

### Calcium (Fig. 2g-i):
- A. capillaris: No change
- P. alpina: No change
- F. vivipara: Ca in leaf sheath 3x greater at elevated CO2 at 65 d; root Ca reduced 36% at 65 d; differences vanished by 189 d

## Summary
- **15 observations** extracted from Table 1 (whole-plant total content)
- 5 elements (N, P, K, Mg, Ca) x 3 species
- A. capillaris: All nutrients increased (plant grew larger at elevated CO2)
- P. alpina: Most nutrients increased or unchanged; Ca decreased
- F. vivipara: All nutrients decreased (plant grew less at elevated CO2)
- Key insight: Changes in total content reflect growth changes, not systematic concentration shifts
