# Lieffering et al. 2004 - Elemental Concentration Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: The impact of elevated CO2 on the elemental concentrations of field-grown rice grains
- **Species**: Oryza sativa L. (rice), cv. Akitakomachi
- **Experiment**: FACE (Free-Air CO2 Enrichment), Shizukuishi, Iwate, Japan
- **CO2 levels**: Ambient + 200 umol/mol (actual: ~625 ppm in 1999, ~570 ppm in 2000)
- **N rate**: 90 kg N/ha (recommended rate)
- **Tissue**: Brown rice grain (dehulled)
- **Replicates**: n=4 (4 FACE rings, 4 ambient plots)
- **Data source**: Figure 1 (bar charts, values estimated)

## Key Findings
- Only N showed significant decrease under elevated CO2 in both years
- No other elements showed significant decreases
- Some elements (Zn, Mn, Fe, B) tended to increase (non-significantly for most)
- Authors attribute lack of dilution to field conditions with ample nutrient supply

## Notes
- All data estimated from Figure 1 bar charts (no tables with numeric values in paper)
- Figure 1 shows macro-elements (N, P, K, Mg, S) in g/kg and micro-elements (Zn, Mn, Fe, Cu, B, Mo, Se) in mg/kg
- Figure 2 shows total amounts removed (g/m2 and mg/m2) combining 1999+2000 - not used for concentration extraction
- 1999 significance pattern (from figure): N(*), P(ns), K(ns), Mg(ns), S(*), Zn(*), Mn(ns), Fe(ns), Cu(ns), B(*), Mo(ns), Se(ns)
- 2000 significance pattern: N(*), P(ns), K(ns), Mg(ns), S(ns), Zn(ns), Mn(ns), Fe(*), Cu(ns), B(ns), Mo(ns), Se(*)
- Ca and Na were mentioned in methods as being measured but do NOT appear in Figure 1 results
- Grain yield increased ~14% under elevated CO2 in both years
- Total observations: 24 (12 elements x 2 years)
