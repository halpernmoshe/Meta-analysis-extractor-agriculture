# 017_Fangmeier_2002 - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Effects of elevated CO2 and/or ozone on nutrient concentrations and nutrient uptake of potatoes
- **Authors**: A. Fangmeier, L. De Temmerman, C. Black, K. Persson, V. Vorne
- **Journal**: European Journal of Agronomy 17 (2002) 353-368
- **Species**: Solanum tuberosum (potato, cv. Bintje)
- **Experiment**: Open-top field chambers (OTC), multi-site CHIP project across Europe
- **Design**: CO2 x O3 factorial (NF, 680, O3, 680O3)
- **CO2 levels**: Ambient (NF) ~370-404 ul/l; Elevated (680) ~680-708 ul/l
- **Sites**: Jokioinen Finland, Tervuren Belgium, Giessen Germany, Goteborg Sweden, Sutton Bonington UK

## Data Sources
- **Table 4**: Interactive effects at intermediate harvest (maximum leaf area) - concentrations and uptake
- **Table 5**: Interactive effects at final harvest (maturity) - concentrations and uptake
- Only CONCENTRATION data extracted (not uptake)
- NF vs 680 = CO2 effect without ozone; O3 vs 680O3 = CO2 effect with ozone background

## Extraction Summary
- **Total observations**: 59
- **Elements**: N, P, K, Ca, Mg, Mn, Zn, Fe (8 elements)
- **Tissues**: Aboveground biomass (AGB), Tuber
- **Harvests**: Intermediate (max leaf area), Final (maturity)
- **Factorial**: Each element x tissue x harvest x ozone level = separate observation

## Key Findings from Paper
- CO2 enrichment generally decreased nutrient concentrations in both AGB and tubers
- N and K reductions most significant and consistent
- Ca in tubers significantly reduced at intermediate harvest (dilution effect from enhanced tuber growth)
- Fe in tubers showed large decrease under CO2 alone but significant CO2 x O3 interaction
- At final harvest, N concentration in tubers reduced ~9.5% (tuber quality concern)
- O3 effects were less consistent than CO2 effects on concentrations
