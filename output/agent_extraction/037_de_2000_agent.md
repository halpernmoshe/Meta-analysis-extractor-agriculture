# Extraction Report: 037_de_2000

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Responses to Iron Limitation in Hordeum vulgare L. as Affected by the Atmospheric CO2 Concentration
- **Authors**: Haase, Rothe, Kania, Wasaki, Romheld, Engels, Kandeler, Neumann (2008)
- **Journal**: Journal of Environmental Quality, 37:1254-1262
- **Note**: File labeled as "037_de_2000" but is actually Haase et al. 2008

## Experimental Design
- **Species**: Barley (Hordeum vulgare L. cv. Europa)
- **CO2 levels**: 400 umol/mol (ambient) vs 800 umol/mol (elevated)
- **Growth conditions**: Growth chambers, 16/8 h day/night, 25/20C, 60% RH
- **Culture systems**: (1) Hydroponic, (2) Soil (rhizobox with calcareous loess subsoil)
- **Fe treatments**: +Fe (20 umol Fe-EDTA) and -Fe (no Fe)
- **Replicates**: n=5 per treatment
- **Harvest**: 16, 22, 28 DAS (hydroponic); 28 DAS (soil)
- **Design**: 2x2 factorial (CO2 x Fe supply)

## Elements Measured
Only **Fe (iron)** was measured as a mineral element in plant tissue.

No other mineral concentrations (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, Zn, Mn, Cu, S, etc.) were reported.

## Data Extraction

### Fe Concentration (Fig. 3b) - Soil culture, shoot, 28 DAS
This is the key data for mineral concentration meta-analysis.

| Fe Supply | Ambient CO2 (mmol/kg DW) | Elevated CO2 (mmol/kg DW) | Effect (%) |
|-----------|--------------------------|---------------------------|------------|
| +Fe       | ~3.5                     | ~3.0                      | -14.3%     |
| -Fe       | ~2.2                     | ~2.0                      | -9.1%      |

**ANOVA result**: CO2 effect on Fe concentration: F(1,16) = 0.7, NS (not significant)

### Fe Content (Fig. 3a) - Soil culture, shoot, 28 DAS
Total Fe per shoot (not concentration).

| Fe Supply | Ambient CO2 (umol/shoot) | Elevated CO2 (umol/shoot) | Effect (%) |
|-----------|--------------------------|---------------------------|------------|
| +Fe       | ~1.5                     | ~2.2                      | +47%       |
| -Fe       | ~1.0                     | ~1.5                      | +52%*      |

**ANOVA result**: CO2 effect on Fe content: F(1,16) = 11.0, P<0.01

*Note: Fe CONTENT increased due to greater biomass, but Fe CONCENTRATION did not change significantly - classic dilution effect.*

## Key Findings
1. Elevated CO2 increased biomass in both Fe-sufficient and Fe-deficient plants
2. Fe concentration in shoots was NOT significantly affected by CO2 (ANOVA P>0.05)
3. Fe content (total per shoot) increased 47-52% under elevated CO2 due to more biomass
4. Phytosiderophore exudation increased 74% under elevated CO2 in Fe-deficient hydroponic plants
5. No other mineral elements were measured in this study
