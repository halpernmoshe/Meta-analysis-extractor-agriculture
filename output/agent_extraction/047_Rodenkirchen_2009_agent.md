# Extraction: Rodenkirchen et al. 2009 (Weigt et al. 2011)

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Effects of twice-ambient carbon dioxide and nitrogen amendment on biomass, nutrient contents and carbon costs of Norway spruce seedlings as influenced by mycorrhization with Piloderma croceum and Tomentellopsis submollis
- **Journal**: Mycorrhiza (2011) 21:375-391
- **Species**: Picea abies (Norway spruce)
- **CO2 levels**: aCO2 ~400 ppm, eCO2 ~700 ppm (aCO2 + 300 ppm)
- **Design**: Factorial - 2 CO2 x 2 N (no N, +N) x 2 fungal species (P. croceum, T. submollis)
- **n**: 10 rhizotrons per treatment
- **Duration**: 13 weeks in greenhouse rhizotrons

## Data Sources
- **Figure 1** (pp. 7-8): Nutrient CONCENTRATIONS (ug/g) in shoot, root, and total plant for 10 elements (N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S, Cu, Zn, Fe, Mn). Bar charts with 4 treatments per tissue x species combination. Values estimated from figure.
- **Table 1** (p. 5): Biomass (dry matter) and C/N ratios - NOT nutrient concentrations
- **Table 4** (p. 9): Total nutrient CONTENTS per rhizotron (ug per seedling trio) - not concentrations

## Extraction Summary
- **Total observations**: 120
- **Elements**: N, P, K, Ca, Mg, S, Cu, Zn, Fe, Mn (10 elements)
- **Tissues**: shoot, root, total plant (3 tissues)
- **Species comparisons**: 2 (P. croceum, T. submollis)
- **CO2 comparisons**: 2 (eCO2 vs aCO2 without N; eCO2+N vs aCO2+N)
- **Observations per element**: 12 (3 tissues x 2 fungal species x 2 N treatments)

## Key Findings
- **P. croceum**: Most nutrients showed DECREASES under eCO2, consistent with dilution effect
  - Strongest decreases: Zn (shoot -38 to -45%), Cu (shoot -27 to -50%), P (shoot -21 to -26%), Ca (shoot -27 to -29%), S (shoot -19 to -30%), Fe (shoot -29 to -31%)
  - Moderate decreases: K (-9 to -13%), Mg (-15 to -20%), Mn (-7 to -11%)
  - N relatively unaffected in shoots (0 to +6%)
- **T. submollis**: Much weaker effects, mostly near zero
  - Some small increases: Zn shoot +25%, Ca root +22-26%, Mn shoot +13%
  - Few decreases observed

## Notes
- All concentration data from Figure 1 (bar charts) - values are estimates
- The paper emphasizes that nutrient UPTAKE did not keep pace with growth under eCO2
- P. croceum promoted more biomass growth but diluted nutrients more strongly
- T. submollis showed less growth promotion and more stable nutrient concentrations
- Significant treatment effects on Zn content under eCO2+N for P. croceum seedlings
