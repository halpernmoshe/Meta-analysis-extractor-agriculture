# Schenk et al. 1997 - Data Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: The response of perennial ryegrass/white clover mini-swards to elevated atmospheric CO2 concentrations: effects on yield and fodder quality
- **Species**: Lolium perenne (perennial ryegrass) / Trifolium repens (white clover)
- **CO2 levels**: 380 ppm (ambient) vs 670 ppm (elevated)
- **Design**: Open-top chambers, 75:25 ryegrass/clover mixture, 1992 and 1993 seasons
- **n**: 8 (4 replicates x 2 duplicates, pooled)

## Data Source
All concentration data from **Table 1** (mg/g DM). Sowing mixture 75:25 only (the agriculturally recommended mixture).

Three tissue categories per element per year:
- Perennial ryegrass
- White clover
- Total yield (mixed sward)

## Notes
- Table 3 contains nutrient *yields* (mg/pot, i.e., total removal by cutting) -- NOT concentrations. These were not extracted as they conflate yield increase with concentration change.
- Figure 3 shows relative CO2 effect on nutrient removal (yield-based), not concentration.
- Figure 4 shows crude fibre, crude protein, ash content over time -- these are not individual mineral elements.
- The paper notes that Ca increase in total yield is driven by higher white clover proportion (not higher Ca per se in either species).
- Na shows the largest and most consistent decline across all tissues and years.
- K also shows consistent declines.
- N declines strongly in ryegrass but not clover; total yield N is mixed due to compositional shifts.
