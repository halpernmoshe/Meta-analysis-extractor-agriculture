# 005_Niinemets_1999 - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Interactive effects of nitrogen and phosphorus on the acclimation potential of foliage photosynthetic properties of cork oak, Quercus suber, to elevated atmospheric CO2 concentrations
- **Authors**: Niinemets, Tenhunen, Canta, Chaves, Faria, Pereira, Reynolds
- **Journal**: Global Change Biology (1999) 5, 455-470
- **Species**: Quercus suber (cork oak)
- **Experiment**: Greenhouse, 21 months
- **CO2 levels**: 350 (ambient) vs 700 (elevated) umol mol-1
- **N treatments**: High (0.3 mol N m-3) vs Low (0.05 mol N m-3)
- **Factorial design**: 2 CO2 x 2 N = 4 treatments
- **n per treatment**: 4-6 plants

## Data Source
All mineral concentration data from **Table 1a** (Foliage mineral concentrations, per unit total dry mass).

Table 1a also reports P/N molar ratio but this is a derived ratio, not an element concentration. Field data row excluded (not part of CO2 experiment).

No additional mineral/element data found in figures (figures show photosynthesis parameters, chlorophyll, and regressions, not mineral concentrations).

## Summary
- **10 observations** total (5 elements x 2 N levels)
- All elements show **negative effects** (decline under elevated CO2)
- P shows the largest decline (-53% at high N, -40% at low N)
- Ca at high N shows essentially no change (-0.5%)
- CO2 effect on mineral concentrations was statistically significant (P<0.01 to P<0.06) for N, P, Ca, Mg, K per ANOVA
- The P/N ratio also declined, indicating P became relatively more limiting at elevated CO2
- No other elements (Fe, Zn, Mn, Cu, S, B, etc.) were measured in this study
