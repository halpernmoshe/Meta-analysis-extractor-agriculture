# 028_Mishra_2011 - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Elevated CO2 affects plant responses to variation in boron availability
- **Authors**: Mishra, Heckathorn, Frantz
- **Journal**: Plant and Soil (2012) 350:117-130
- **Species**: Pelargonium x hortorum (geranium cv. Maverick White)
- **CO2 levels**: 370 ppm (ambient) vs 700 ppm (elevated)
- **Duration**: 30 days
- **Design**: Factorial: 3 B levels (4.5, 45, 450 uM) x 2 CO2 levels
- **Replicates**: n=3 per treatment
- **Other species**: Barley (Hordeum vulgare) and Azolla caroliniana also studied but nutrient concentration data only shown for geranium

## Data Sources
- **Figure 3a,b**: Boron [B] concentration in leaf and root (mg/kg dry mass)
- **Figure 4a**: Phosphorus [P] in leaf and root (mg/kg)
- **Figure 4b**: Zinc [Zn] in root (mg/kg)
- **Figure 4c**: Copper [Cu] in root (mg/kg)
- **Figure 4d**: Iron [Fe] in root (mg/kg)
- **Table 2**: ANOVA p-values only (no means)
- **Text notes**: CO2 affected most nutrients; only those with significant B x CO2 interactions shown in figures

## Notes on Data Availability
- The paper explicitly states: "we restrict presentation of results here to nutrients that were affected by B or for which there were significant B x CO2 interactions in either leaves or roots"
- Text mentions: "elevated CO2 affected the concentration of nutrients in most instances here (all but P in roots and shoots, Mg and S in roots, and Fe and Zn in shoots; not shown)"
- B affected [P] in leaves (decreasing at 45 uM B, P=0.0078)
- [Fe] highest at 450 uM B (P=0.0279)
- [Zn] in roots highest at 450 uM B in ambient CO2 only (P=0.0188)
- Cu in roots: significant B x CO2 interaction (P=0.0100)
- Zn in roots: marginally significant B x CO2 (P=0.0655)

## Summary
- **21 observations** extracted across 5 elements (B, P, Cu, Fe, Zn), 2 tissues (leaf, root), 3 B levels
- All values estimated from figures (no numerical data tables in paper)
- B consistently decreased in both leaves and roots under elevated CO2
- B decrease was larger in leaves than roots
- B decrease was significant only at 450 uM B level in leaves (text: 55% reduction)
- Other nutrient responses varied by B level, showing complex B x CO2 interactions
- Cu and Fe tended to increase in roots at low-medium B under elevated CO2
- Zn decreased in roots at high B under elevated CO2 (significant interaction)
