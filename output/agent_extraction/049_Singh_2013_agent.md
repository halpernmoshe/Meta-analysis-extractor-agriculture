# 049_Singh_2013 - Agent Extraction

## Paper Details
- **Title**: Synergistic action of tropospheric ozone and carbon dioxide on yield and nutritional quality of Indian mustard (*Brassica juncea* (L.) Czern.)
- **Species**: *Brassica juncea* (Indian mustard, var. Pusa Tarak EJ-13)
- **Experiment type**: Open-top chamber (OTC), field experiment
- **Location**: Indian Agricultural Research Institute, New Delhi, India
- **Years**: 2009-10 and 2010-11 (rabi/winter seasons)
- **CO2 levels**: Ambient (~385 ppm) vs Elevated (500 +/- 50 ppm)
- **Replicates**: 3

## Experimental Design
Six treatments:
1. NF - Nonfiltered air (ambient O3, ambient CO2) **[CONTROL]**
2. CF - Charcoal-filtered air (low O3, ambient CO2)
3. EO - Elevated ozone (NF + 25-35 ppb O3)
4. EO+CO2 - Elevated ozone + elevated CO2
5. NF+CO2 - Nonfiltered air + elevated CO2 **[TREATMENT]**
6. AC - Ambient air control (no chamber)

## CO2 Effect Comparison: NF+CO2 vs NF
This is the cleanest comparison for isolating the CO2 effect (both at ambient ozone).

## Summary
- **14 observations** extracted (7 elements x 2 years)
- Elements: Oil, Protein, Ca, Mg, S, Zn, Fe
- Oil increased under elevated CO2 (~6-7%)
- Protein decreased under elevated CO2 (~5-6%)
- All minerals (Ca, Mg, S, Zn, Fe) decreased under elevated CO2 (5-12%)
- Consistent direction across both years
- Results align with known CO2 dilution effect on mineral nutrients

## Notes
- Paper also reports N content indirectly (protein = N x 6.25), but protein is the reported metric
- No P, K, Cu, Mn, B, Na, Mo, Si, or C data reported
- Table 4 header states Ca/Mg/S in mg/g and Zn/Fe in mg/kg
- Data from figures (Figs 1-2) cover ozone concentrations and photosynthetic rates, not mineral data
